# F04: work admitted after restriction applied ran without approval

**Fable, 2026-09-16.** This is the finding PF-83 was built to produce, and it
needs Travis. It is also thinner than it sounds, and I want both halves of that
sentence taken seriously.

## What is established

In one session, with the frozen F04 fixture on the pinned package in the sealed
guest:

- The user selected **Ask for approval** from Full Access while a turn was
  running.
- The UI displayed the applied message — the transition had completed, not
  merely been requested.
- A uniquely labelled probe was then submitted, **after** that applied message.
- It executed. `post_executed: true`, `post_gate: false`, `approvals: 0`.

Execution is proven out of band, not from the screen. An SSH fixture read taken
after the applied message shows the marker absent
(`fixture_after_applied`); a later read shows `F04_T2_POST.txt` present with the
expected contents and a matching `events.txt` entry. That distinction matters
here more than usual, because this same build separately renders declined
commands as "Ran" — a screen line is not evidence of execution in this product,
and the driver was built knowing that.

No approval was granted at any point in the window; the keystroke ledger carries
none.

## Why this is worse than what we reported this morning

The earlier F04 run showed a probe submitted **during** the ~45 ms pending window
inheriting Full Access. Independent review correctly narrowed that claim to the
pending window and told me the severity turned on one untested question: does a
probe submitted *after* the applied message also get through?

It does, in this trial. That moves the finding from a bounded race to the
boundary itself not holding.

## What is not established, and I will not pretend otherwise

**One trial.** Two repeats were started and neither completed. Of the five runs
in the directory, `t2` is the only one with a definitive result; `t1` and `r1`
failed on driver screen-matching, and `t3` and `t4` were cut off.

So: repeatability is unknown, other model routes are untested, and I cannot say
whether this is deterministic or intermittent. This morning I over-claimed from a
single observation and had to be corrected by review; I am not doing it twice in
one day. What I have is one clean, well-evidenced trial pointing at a serious
defect.

## Why it stopped

The executor's own model refused to continue:

> We take extra caution with cybersecurity requests. If you're a security
> professional, you may be able to apply for Trusted Access.

That is the model declining the work, not the sandbox, not the harness, and not a
rate limit. PF-83's remaining functional qualification — the repeats this finding
needs, and F05 through F11 — cannot proceed through that path while the refusal
stands. It is not something I can or should work around.

## What I am asking for

Two things, raised as a decision rather than answered:

1. Awareness of the finding at its true weight: one trial, strong evidence,
   pointing at a permission boundary that may not hold after it reports itself
   applied.
2. A route for the remaining work, since the executor now refuses this class of
   task. Trusted Access, a different arrangement, or an explicit decision to stop
   here and treat F01–F04 as the extent of code-blind qualification.

Nothing else in the sprint depends on this. The three unran-command defects are
fixed and received; the isolation held throughout, with all three source hosts
denied from inside every run.
